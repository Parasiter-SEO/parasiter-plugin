# Author intro and hook

The internet is faceless: product pages, AI answers, no-name reviews. Buyers look for a person whose opinion they can rely on — an expert, a tester, a real buyer. The intro is where the reader decides "is this a real person worth listening to?" If the answer is no, they leave in seconds.

## What the author block must answer

1. **Who** — name (real or the pen name the user publishes under).
2. **What I do / my position** — role, field, years in it.
3. **Why trust me** — what qualifies me to judge this product: I used it for N weeks; I compared N products; I work with buyers of this category; I studied the packages, terms and complaints.
4. **Why listen to me now** — what this article gives the reader that the product page and AI answers do not.

Every element comes from the user's author profile. No invented degrees, jobs or tests.

## Hook formula: author → problem → solution → bridge → promise

- **Author**: 1–2 sentences.
- **Problem**: the reader's real situation in their words.
- **Solution**: what actually solves it (the product or the right choice).
- **Bridge**: how to get from the problem to the solution — what to check, what to pick.
- **Promise**: what this article covers and what the reader will know by the end.

## Examples (adapt; use only true facts)

Expert comparison:
> I'm Mark, and I've spent the last 8 years helping people choose nutrition products. For this guide I compared 5 weight-loss supplements side by side: ingredients, real package prices, refund terms and hundreds of buyer complaints. If you're tired of paying for products that don't fit you, below I'll show which one makes sense for which situation, what each really costs, and where the best deal is this month.

Personal experience (only if real):
> My name is Anna. I'm 35, and after two years of trying to fix my sleep I decided to test [Product] myself. I kept notes for 60 days. Here is what I paid, what changed, what didn't, and whether I'd buy it again.

Research-based (no hands-on use):
> I'm [Name], I review [category] tools for small online stores. I haven't used [Product] long-term, so this guide doesn't pretend to: I compared its plans, limits, refund policy and what real users report, against two alternatives.

## Forum / Reddit tone

Native, first person, friendly, no sales voice:
> Hey everyone, Gary here — I post reviews on this forum now and then. I've tried a bunch of products in this category and five of them actually impressed me. Here's my honest breakdown, would love to hear your experience too.

## Mistakes

- Starting with product features (that info is everywhere already).
- A generic "As an expert with 10 years of experience…" with no specifics.
- An author who is not mentioned again: refer back to the author's view in each product section and in the verdict.
- An intro that promises something the page does not deliver.
