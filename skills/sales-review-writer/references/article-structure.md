# Article structures

All types start with the shared opening from SKILL.md Step 3:
Title → First image → Quick facts box (line-by-line specs + current deals + main link) → Author intro + hook → Table of contents.

Readers behave differently: some scan and buy from the top, some read everything, some research for days. The structure must sell to all of them, not filter them.

## Quick facts box (line by line)

Single product:
```
[Product] at a glance (checked: [date])
• What it is: …
• Best for: …
• Key feature: …
• Format / size / plan: …
• Price: from $… · best value: [bundle] at $…/unit
• Current deal: [discount / bonus / free shipping] — until [date if stated]
• Guarantee / refund: …
→ [CTA to the order page]
```

Best-of: the same box as a compact ranking — one line per product: position, name, best for, price from, current deal, link. #1 visually marked.

## Single-product review

1. Shared opening.
2. The problem and why people look for this product.
3. What [Product] is and how it works — short; facts only.
4. Key features / ingredients / components.
5. Benefits — tied to the reader's problem, not a feature list.
6. My experience or my research — what I checked and what I found.
7. Pros and cons — honest; include real cons.
8. Who it is for / who should not buy.
9. Alternatives — 2–3 products and when each is the better choice (always compare, even in a single review).
10. Packages and prices — which bundle to pick and why; current deal; CTA.
11. FAQ — questions buyers really ask (SERP, "People also ask", support, forums).
12. Verdict — the author's clear recommendation + main CTA.
13. Affiliate disclosure + last-checked date.

## Best-of comparison (5, 7 or 10 products)

"Best" articles usually sell several times more than single reviews: rankings simplify the decision. They work when they are a choice tool, not a list.

1. Shared opening (quick facts = ranking summary).
2. How I chose and compared — criteria and method.
3. Comparison table: product, best for, key feature, price from, guarantee, current deal.
4. One section per product, same template every time so readers learn the pattern:
   - overview · key features / ingredients · benefits · pros and cons · my experience or research notes · best for · deal + CTA.
5. Choice scenarios: "Pick A if…, B if…, C if…".
6. FAQ.
7. Verdict: #1 and why, runner-up, budget pick; CTA.
8. Disclosure + last-checked date.

A less-known product with a strong commission can be placed high only if it is genuinely a good fit and its offer page converts — the article must justify the position honestly.

## A vs B

1. Shared opening: who should pick A, who should pick B — in the first 100 words.
2. Table: price, contents, design/features, shipping, guarantee — verified facts only.
3. Practical differences in setup and use.
4. Weaknesses of each.
5. Scenarios: "Choose A if… / B if…".
6. CTA next to each decision.
7. FAQ, disclosure, last-checked date.

## Personal experience / journal (only with real experience)

1. Shared opening.
2. What I tested, when, how long, under which conditions.
3. Photos, notes, starting parameters — no exaggeration.
4. Timeline: what changed, what didn't.
5. What was convenient and inconvenient; what didn't work.
6. Observations vs conclusions — keep them apart.
7. Who my experience may not apply to.
8. Verdict + link to the exact package used.

## Complaints / "is it legit"

1. State the risk the buyer is checking.
2. Separate confirmed facts, individual complaints and opinion.
3. Common causes of problems and how to check before buying.
4. When NOT to buy.
5. Honest alternatives and transparent links.

## Packages / price guide

1. Every package and what's inside.
2. Real cost per unit and total cost incl. shipping.
3. Which package fits which buyer.
4. Current deal, guarantee, where to buy safely (official seller check).
5. CTA to the exact package page.

## Headings rule

H2/H3 headings read alone must tell the story and the conclusion. Bad: "Overview", "Features". Good: "Why [Product] Works Best for Night-Time Cravings", "The 6-Bottle Pack Is the Real Bargain — Here's the Math".
