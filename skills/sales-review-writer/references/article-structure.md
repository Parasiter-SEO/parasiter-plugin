# Article structures

All types start with the shared opening from SKILL.md Step 3:
Title → First image → Quick facts box (line-by-line specs + current deals + main link) → Author intro + hook → Table of contents.

Readers behave differently: some scan and buy from the top, some read everything, some research for days. The structure must sell to all of them, not filter them.

Every template below contains the five mandatory blocks from SKILL.md: **pains → product facts and how it solves them → life after → prices and current offers → comparison and natural alternatives**. Minimum 1,500 words for every article.

## Pains (how to write them)

- 3–5 pains, each in the buyer's words, as they would type them into Google or a forum ("I've tried three shampoos and still see hair on my pillow every morning").
- For each: what it costs them (money already spent, time, embarrassment, health worry, missed chances) and what they already tried and why it didn't work.
- Keep it real and specific; no fear-mongering, no invented statistics.

## Product facts → solution

For each pain, one short block:
```
Pain: …
What [Product] does about it: … (fact from the merchant / user, with source)
Why that matters for you: …
```
End with a one-paragraph summary of how the product works as a whole.

## Life after

Concrete scenes of the reader's life once the problem is handled: morning routine, work, money, relationships, confidence, free time. Show what changes and why it improves their life. Phrase as what the product is designed to help with and what users typically report ("most buyers say…", only from real sources), with honest limits and timeframes. No guaranteed results, no before/after promises.

## Prices and current offers (dedicated block)

```
Prices and deals (checked: [date])
| Package | What's inside | Price | Per unit | Notes |
Best value: [package] — why
Current deal: [discount / coupon / bonus / free shipping] — until [date if stated]
Guarantee: …
→ [CTA to the exact package page]
```

## Comparison and natural alternatives

- Table: [Product] vs 2–3 competitors — price, key feature, format, guarantee, best for.
- "Choose [Product] if… / choose [Competitor] if…".
- **Natural alternatives**: lifestyle changes, home or natural remedies, free tools or methods — what each realistically gives, when it is enough, and when the product is the better choice. Honest alternatives make the recommendation believable.

## Quick facts box (line by line)

Single product:
```
[Product] at a glance (checked: [date])
• What it is: …
• Best for: …
• Key feature: …
• Format / size / plan: …
• Price: from $… · best value: [bundle] at $…/unit
• Current deal: [discount / bonus / free shipping] — until [date if stated]
• Guarantee / refund: …
→ [CTA to the order page]
```

Best-of: the same box as a compact ranking — one line per product: position, name, best for, price from, current deal, link. #1 visually marked.

## Single-product review

1. Shared opening.
2. Pains — 3–5 real pains and what they cost the reader.
3. What [Product] is and how it solves each pain — facts only, pain by pain.
4. Key features / ingredients / components.
5. Life after — how the reader's days change once the problem is handled.
6. My experience or my research — what I checked and what I found.
7. Pros and cons — honest; include real cons.
8. Who it is for / who should not buy.
9. Comparison and natural alternatives — 2–3 competing products (table + "choose X if…") and non-product options.
10. Prices and current offers — every package, best value, current deal; CTA.
11. FAQ — questions buyers really ask (SERP, "People also ask", support, forums).
12. Verdict — the author's clear recommendation + main CTA.
13. Affiliate disclosure + last-checked date.

## Best-of comparison (10, 14 or 15 products by default)

"Best" articles usually sell several times more than single reviews: rankings simplify the decision. They work when they are a choice tool, not a list.

1. Shared opening (quick facts = ranking summary).
2. Pains — what buyers in this category struggle with; each product section later says which pain it solves best.
3. How I chose and compared — criteria and method.
3. Comparison table: product, best for, key feature, price from, guarantee, current deal.
4. One section per product, same template every time so readers learn the pattern:
   - overview · key features / ingredients · benefits · pros and cons · my experience or research notes · best for · deal + CTA.
5. Life after — what changes for the reader with the right pick.
6. Choice scenarios: "Pick A if…, B if…, C if…".
7. Natural alternatives — non-product options and when they are enough.
8. Prices and current offers for the top picks.
9. FAQ.
10. Verdict: #1 and why, runner-up, budget pick; CTA.
11. Disclosure + last-checked date.

A less-known product with a strong commission can be placed high only if it is genuinely a good fit and its offer page converts — the article must justify the position honestly.

## A vs B

1. Shared opening: who should pick A, who should pick B — in the first 100 words.
2. Pains both products address, and which pain each handles better.
3. Table: price, contents, design/features, shipping, guarantee — verified facts only.
4. Practical differences in setup and use.
5. Weaknesses of each.
6. Life after with the right pick.
7. Scenarios: "Choose A if… / B if…" + natural alternatives.
8. Prices and current offers for both; CTA next to each decision.
9. FAQ, disclosure, last-checked date.

## Personal experience / journal (only with real experience)

1. Shared opening.
2. What I tested, when, how long, under which conditions.
3. Photos, notes, starting parameters — no exaggeration.
4. Timeline: what changed, what didn't.
5. What was convenient and inconvenient; what didn't work.
6. Observations vs conclusions — keep them apart.
7. Who my experience may not apply to.
8. Verdict + link to the exact package used.

## Complaints / "is it legit"

1. State the risk the buyer is checking, and the pain behind the purchase.
2. Separate confirmed facts, individual complaints and opinion.
3. What the product actually does for that pain (facts).
4. Common causes of problems and how to check before buying.
5. When NOT to buy.
6. Prices, refund terms and safe place to buy.
7. Comparison with 2–3 alternatives + natural alternatives, transparent links.

## Packages / price guide

1. The pain that brought the buyer here and what the product does about it (short).
2. Every package and what's inside.
3. Real cost per unit and total cost incl. shipping.
4. Which package fits which buyer, and the life-after picture for that choice.
5. Current deal, guarantee, where to buy safely (official seller check).
6. Short comparison of price per unit with 1–2 competitors where it helps the decision.
7. CTA to the exact package page.

## Headings rule

H2/H3 headings read alone must tell the story and the conclusion. Bad: "Overview", "Features". Good: "Why [Product] Works Best for Night-Time Cravings", "The 6-Bottle Pack Is the Real Bargain — Here's the Math".
