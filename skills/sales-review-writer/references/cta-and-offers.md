# CTAs, offers and triggers

A visit to the merchant's site is not a purchase. The reader must arrive already knowing what to buy and why now.

## The operator's homework (AI cannot do it)

Before writing CTAs, the user opens the merchant/order page and records:
- all packages and prices, cost per unit;
- current discount, coupon, bonus items, free shipping;
- guarantee / refund terms;
- whether the offer has a stated end date;
- the best landing URL: homepage vs order/bundle page (the order page often converts better).

Write these into the quick facts box and the CTAs, with the date checked.

## CTA formula

**Why this product + why this offer + why now + exact action.**

Weak: "Click here to visit the official website."
Strong: "Right now the 6-bottle pack is $[X] per bottle with free shipping and a [N]-day refund window — the lowest price I've seen on it. If [problem] is what brought you here, this is the pack I'd pick. → Check the current 6-bottle price"

Button/link texts: "Check Current Package", "See Today's Price", "View Official Offer", "Get the [N]-Pack Deal".

## Placement

- The product name itself is a link (quick facts, first mention per section, tables, verdict).

- Quick facts box at the top (many buy right here).
- After each product section in best-of articles.
- Next to each choice scenario ("Pick A if…").
- In the verdict.
- Not in every paragraph; never as a wall of buttons.

## Motivation triggers (truthful only)

- Real current deal and its real end date.
- Price math: cost per day/unit, bundle savings.
- Risk removal: guarantee, refund, official seller.
- The author's personal choice ("this is the pack I'd buy").
- Social proof only from real sources (with the source named).

Invented deadlines, fake scarcity, fake "only today" and fabricated testimonials are not allowed: they break trust, platform rules and advertising law.

## Platform tone

- Forums, Reddit, PDF on official sites: soft, native CTAs ("here's the official page if you want to check the bundles"). No "BUY NOW".
- Press releases on GlobeNewswire/Yahoo Finance: neutral, educational; avoid "best" and hype words.
- Medium/LinkedIn/Raisely/blogs: full CTA formula.
- YouTube description / Facebook posts: link and the deal in the first 1–3 lines.
