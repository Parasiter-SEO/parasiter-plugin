# Rumer's concepts: Web 2.0, Web 3.0 and Creative SEO

These are **Rumer's own terms**. Elsewhere on the internet "Web 2.0" usually means any site where users publish (Medium, Blogger, Tumblr…) and "Web3" means blockchain. In PARASITER they mean what is written below. The first time one of them comes up in a session, say "Web 2.0 by Rumer" (or Web 3.0, or Creative SEO) and give the one-line meaning.

Every skill that uses these terms points here. Short versions for users are in `catalog.md` ("Rumer's terms"), `faq.md` and `how-to.md`.

## Platform classes

Every platform in PARASITER (in searches, in the analyzer's verdict, in the user's own base) belongs to one of three classes.

| Class | What it is (Rumer's definition) | How PARASITER finds it | What the user does with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **proven** | Affiliate reviews already rank there now. Everyone can see it and anyone can post there. | The checked base, the live "[product] review" scan, the "follow" track of the pattern search. | Publish now, test 1–3 posts. Expect competition and cleanup waves. |
| **Web 2.0 🔒** | A platform that other SEOs barely use. You are its discoverer: only you know it exists, you work it alone, and you keep it out of sight of other SEOs by keeping it in your personal platform list for your own use. | The "clean" track of the pattern search (good signs, open sign-up, no affiliate posts yet). Also the analyzer when a platform has no affiliate reviews but passes sign-up, indexing and topic. | Test plan (neutral post → review 2–3 days later → indexing check). If it works, save it with `--class web2` and follow the privacy rules below. |
| **Web 3.0** | A platform everyone can see: its pages sit in the top, but nobody knows how to publish on it, because the whole process is the barrier. Rumer's example is Amazon S3 (AWS). Everyone sees its pages in the top, but few know how the whole process works. | Domains whose pages rank for the niche's commercial queries but where no sign-up or post form exists, because publishing goes through the service's own tools (cloud and hosting services, developer and data services, app or document stores). | Ask "how do people publish on <domain>?". The analyzer's Web 3.0 mode researches the legitimate route from the service's own documentation, along with its cost, indexing and risks. Then one test page. |

**Life cycle.** A Web 2.0 platform becomes proven once other people find it and start posting there. A Web 3.0 platform becomes proven once its route spreads. A proven platform gets spammed and then cleaned up. Being first on a platform is worth most and lasts shortest. That is why PARASITER keeps searching for new platforms instead of handing everyone the same list.

### Web 2.0: privacy rules

A Web 2.0 platform stays valuable only while nobody else knows about it.
- **Never name it in public.** That covers groups, chats, comments, screenshots, videos and case studies. When you show your results, blur the domain.
- **Leave no trail.** Don't link your Web 2.0 profiles from your public profiles. Use one account and post naturally. Never mass-post, because a wave of affiliate posts gets the platform noticed by both SEOs and moderators.
- **PARASITER keeps it local.** Nothing a search finds is sent to the PARASITER server. Web 2.0 domains are also left out of the Telegram check summary (`pages_report`) even when sharing is on. When the user shares a report or the base with anyone, PARASITER offers to leave the Web 2.0 rows out.
- **When others arrive.** If checks find other people's affiliate posts on a `web2` platform, PARASITER says so and offers to mark it `proven`.

### Web 3.0: rules

- **Legitimate routes only.** The user opens their own account on the service and publishes their own pages, following the service's terms. A route that needs someone else's account, other organisations' storage or folders, or bypassing access controls is abuse. PARASITER never suggests it.
- **Stop list first.** Domains in the PARASITER stop list or blacklist are not shown or researched, not even as Web 3.0. Today the stop list includes Amazon S3 itself, so S3 serves only as the example that explains the term.
- **Risks, every time.** Services close accounts used for spam. Google's site reputation abuse policy and spam rules apply to pages on any host. Keep every page useful, disclose affiliate links, and test with one page before scaling.

## Creative SEO

**Creative SEO** is Rumer's own term and his main working principle: invent non-standard, original ideas in parasite SEO that give new strategies, methods and tools, either ones that are still poorly explored or ones that are completely new. **PARASITER itself is a result of Creative SEO.** No tool like it existed before.

Creative SEO in PARASITER:
- **Web 2.0 search**: the pattern search looks for platforms nobody uses yet (`platform-finder`).
- **Web 3.0 research**: finding the route on a platform everyone sees and nobody uses (`platform-analyzer`, Web 3.0 mode).
- **Creative SEO idea**: on request ("give me a Creative SEO idea", "something nobody does"), or when the standard route stalls (an empty platform search, a saturated niche, a platform cleaned up, a content type that stopped working), any skill may offer 1–3 ideas labelled **Creative SEO**.

How to give a Creative SEO idea:
1. **The idea** in one or two lines: what to do differently.
2. **Why it may work**: what almost nobody does here, and which signal suggests Google or buyers would reward it.
3. **The cheapest test**: 1–3 pages or one platform, with the success measure (indexed, top 20, clicks, sales) and the date to check it.
4. **The risk**: platform rules, Google's policies, time.
5. Label it as a **hypothesis to test**, never as a proven method. Ideas stay within the platform's rules, the law and Google's policies: no hacks, no spam, no hidden links, no other people's accounts.

What worked goes into the user's journal (`work-log`) like any page. Ideas that keep working become the user's own method.
