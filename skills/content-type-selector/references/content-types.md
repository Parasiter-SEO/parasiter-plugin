# Content types that still get clicks and sales (2026)

Buyer journey: discovery → **doubt → comparison → trust check → package/price** → purchase.
The most profitable pages sit in the middle and at the end of this chain. Ordered from strongest to weakest.

| # | Type | Why people click | What you sell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A vs B comparison | Choosing between two products | Both; honest "choose A if… / B if…" |
| 2 | Personal experience / journal | Want real observations, photos, limits | The product actually tested |
| 3 | Complaints / problems / doubts | Checking the risk of a wrong purchase | Solution or a better-fitting alternative |
| 4 | Packages / pricing / discounts | Already decided, choosing the version or deal | The right package, bundle, accessories |
| 5 | Real discussions (forums, Reddit, Quora) | Want several independent opinions | Contextual links where allowed |
| 6 | Interactive selection tool | Want a personal answer | The result of the selection |
| 7 | Original research | Want data and methodology | Indirect: trust, links |
| 8 | "Best of" / top-N / general review | Info is easy for AI to retell | Supporting content; "best of" only as a decision tool |

## 1. A vs B comparison
Buyer has narrowed down to two options and is almost ready. Never "both are great" — give scenarios.
Topic patterns: `A vs B: [criterion]`, `A alternative`, `Standard vs Deluxe`, `Budget vs Premium: total cost`, `Rod vs Belt` (design types).
Structure: title with both products + criterion → first 100 words: who should pick A, who B, why → verified comparison table (price, contents, design, shipping, warranty) → practical differences in use → honest disadvantages of each → "choose A if… / B if…" → CTA next to each decision → real FAQ → last-checked date, affiliate disclosure, sources.

## 2. Personal experience / journal
"My N-month experience", "What I wish I knew before buying", "Setup mistakes I made".
The more specific and personal, the better it converts — **only with real experience.**
Structure: what was tested, when, how long, conditions → own photos/notes/starting point → what was convenient and what was not, what failed → observations vs conclusions → who this experience may not apply to → verdict + link to the specific package.

## 3. Complaints / problems / doubts
Queries: scam, legit, complaints, problems, worth it, side effects (careful), return.
The buyer is afraid of a mistake. Honest answers win trust; the sale goes to the product or a better alternative.
Structure: state the risk briefly → separate confirmed facts / individual complaints / opinion → common causes of problems and how to check before buying → when NOT to buy → honest alternatives with transparent links.

## 4. Packages / pricing / discounts
"What's in the kit", "Standard vs Deluxe", "Shipping and warranty explained", "Total cost", "Discount / coupon / where to buy safely".
Shortest path to the sale; most affiliates ignore it.
Structure: quick answer (which package for whom) → contents checklist per package → total cost incl. shipping → current deals (real, dated) → seller verification / official store → returns and warranty as published → CTA "Check current package / price".

## 5. Real discussions
Forums, Reddit, Quora, community threads. Works when the post genuinely contributes and the platform allows links. Match the platform's style; no disguised ads.

## 6. Interactive selection tool
Quiz, calculator, "which one fits you". Best on own site or platforms that allow interactive pages.

## 7. Original research
Own data, testing methodology, tables. Builds trust and links; converts indirectly. No pseudo-science, no invented data.

## 8. "Best of" / general review
A "best of" that is a real decision tool (table, clear criteria, a scenario per buyer type) still sells — in the author's practice, much better than single reviews.
A top-N that lists products with descriptions copied from official sites is a retelling — AI replaces it. Single general reviews = supporting content, not the main income.
